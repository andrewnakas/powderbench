# Data: sources, truth definition, QC

## Ground truth: SNOTEL (stations league)

The league runs the **full active SNOTEL network** — every station with both a
snow-depth and an SWE sensor and ≥3 years of record (~865), regenerated by
`scripts/networks/snotel.py` into `data/stations/snotel.csv`. The 45 curated
stations in `data/stations.yaml` are the featured (headline) set; regions for
the rest come from the nearest curated zone within 60 km, else the state.

Source: USDA NRCS AWDB REST API
(`https://wcc.sc.egov.usda.gov/awdbRestApi`), public domain, no key.

We fetch **daily** values with `periodRef=END`, so `SNWD(d)` — snow depth,
inches — is the reading at the **end** of station-local day `d` (verified
against hourly data: the daily value equals the 00:00 reading of `d+1`).

**Fresh snowfall attributed to day `d`:**

```
snow24(d) = max(SNWD(d) − SNWD(d−1), 0)
```

This aligns with how forecast models report local-day snowfall totals
(confirmed against the Jan 3–5, 2025 Alta storm on both sources). Settling and
melt make the raw delta negative on no-snow days — clamped to zero. Horizons
are cumulative sums: `h48(D) = snow24(D) + snow24(D+1)`, etc.

Snow depth deltas are a noisy but honest measure of what skiers actually care
about. Its known quirks (settling during storms under-measures very wet storms,
wind loading, sensor noise of ±1") apply to every competitor equally.

## QC: voided station-days

A station-day is voided — excluded from scoring for everyone — when
(`src/powderbench/truth.py`):

| Rule | Condition |
|---|---|
| `missing` | either day's SNWD reading is absent |
| `bad_depth` | depth < 0 or > 300" |
| `implausible_jump` | daily delta > 48" |
| `no_swe_support` | delta ≥ 6" while SWE rose < 0.1" (snow pillow disagrees) |
| `swe_missing_on_big_jump` | delta ≥ 6" but SWE data is missing |

A 48h/72h window is valid only if every component day is valid. Typical
midwinter void rates are a few percent.

Network station names carry the nearest ski area (OpenSkiData, ODbL —
`data/skiareas/`), e.g. SNOTEL "Atwater" → "Alta Ski Area (2 km)".

## Ground truth: measured new snow (observers league)

The observers league scores against **measured** new snowfall — the snowboard
reading NWS COOP and CoCoRaHS volunteers take each morning — from NOAA
**GHCN-Daily** (element `SNOW`, inches), fetched keylessly from the NCEI Access
Data Service. COOP data is US public domain; CoCoRaHS data is CC BY 3.0
(© CoCoRaHS, cocorahs.org).

- **Stations**: `scripts/networks/ghcnd.py` regenerates `data/stations/ghcnd.csv`
  from NCEI's inventory: US stations reporting `SNOW` through the current year
  with records back to ≤2020, at mountain elevations (≥1,500 m west of 100°W,
  ≥500 m east of it — Adirondacks, Greens, Whites, Appalachians; since
  2026-10-21 the western rule also requires a SNOTEL site within 30 km and the
  eastern one longitude east of 93°W, which keeps out the high plains), **plus
  any observer within 15 km of a ski area with 2+ lifts** (OpenSkiData) at any
  elevation — lake-effect and eastern ski towns included. Then — after
  the climatology build — only stations with a solid winter record (an h24
  climatology on ≥60 of the ~90 Dec–Feb days) and a clear day-attribution fit (below). ~1,150 stations.
  **Featured**: the three highest per curated ski zone and per eastern
  ski-country region.
- **Day alignment — fitted per station.** Observers file a reading under the
  date they took it, but read the board at different times (morning observers:
  the snow fell the day before; evening observers: same day). A one-day error
  shifts every storm, so `scripts/networks/*.py` fit each station's
  `day_offset` (local day D ← report dated D+offset) by correlating its last
  two winters of reports against ERA5 daily snowfall at its coordinates for
  shifts −1/0/+1 (`powderbench/networks/attribution.py`). A station is kept
  only if the best shift correlates ≥0.4 **and** beats the runner-up by ≥0.1 —
  stations that don't track storms, or are ambiguous, are dropped. Most US
  observers fit +1 (morning); most ECCC stations fit 0.
- **Canada (ECCC)**: measured `TOTAL_SNOW` (cm) from ECCC's climate-daily OGC
  API (Open Government Licence – Canada), `scripts/networks/eccc.py` →
  `data/stations/eccc.csv` (`truth_source: eccc`). Only 8 mountain stations
  still measure snowfall daily — Whistler Roundhouse, Silver Star, Grouse
  Mountain, Kananaskis and Barkerville are featured. Most automatic stations
  don't measure snowfall; one reported a constant 0 all winter (dropped by the
  fit). QC voids flags M (missing), A (accumulated over several days) and E
  (estimated); trace = 0. ECCC packs station coordinates as DD.MMSS — decoded
  explicitly.
- **QC** (voided for everyone): no report (observers often leave the field
  blank on dry days — blank is never treated as zero), any NCEI quality flag,
  negative values or >48". Trace (`T`) counts as 0.
- **Latency**: CoCoRaHS lands within ~1 day, COOP keyed forms within days — the
  league resolves at D+6. Responses less than 45 days old are never cached, so
  late reports are picked up on re-fetch.

## Ground truth: measured new snow, Swiss and Austrian Alps (alps league)

The alps league scores against **HN — the 24h height of new snow** published by
the WSL Institute for Snow and Avalanche Research **SLF** (CC BY 4.0; data ©
SLF, www.slf.ch), from `measurement-data.slf.ch` (daily-regenerated archive,
records to the 1930s) topped up by the live `measurement-api.slf.ch` for the
last week.

- **Manual study plots** (`:SLF`): an observer's snowboard at ~07:00 local
  (`measure_date` 06:00 UTC) — the gold standard; ~100 plots, featured ones at
  Zermatt, Saas-Fee, Verbier, Crans-Montana, St. Moritz, Davos, Klosters,
  Arosa, Laax, Engelberg, Andermatt, Grindelwald, Mürren, Wengen, Adelboden…
- **IMIS automatic stations** (`:IMIS`): HN_1D derived by SLF from the
  snow-depth sensor; network tier.
- Day attribution fitted per station like the observers league; QC voids
  missing values, SLF's negative sentinels and >48".
- Season Nov–May; cutoff 21:00 UTC on D−1 (before Swiss local midnight in
  both CET and CEST); resolves D+4.

### Austria: GeoSphere Austria climate stations

The Austrian stations (`:GEO`, `truth_source: geosphere`) score against
`shneu_manu`, the 24h new snow measured by hand at the 06 UTC (07:00 CET)
observation. It comes from GeoSphere Austria's daily climate dataset
(`klima-v2-1d`, CC BY 4.0; data © GeoSphere Austria). Updates are daily and
there's no key.

- **Candidates:** active stations at ≥ 900 m with ≥ 100 manual readings last
  winter. GeoSphere lists many sites under two station numbers with identical
  values; one is kept per site.
- **Day attribution:** the 07:00 reading should hold yesterday's snow, but
  fitted against ERA5, stations split. St. Anton fits the same day clearly
  (r = 0.80 vs 0.64), while Obergurgl fits the next morning's report (0.66 vs
  0.27). So every station is fitted with the strict margin rule, no documented
  default. Near-ties are excluded (Warth, Langen am Arlberg, Seefeld and 19
  others), which leaves 22 of 44 sites.
- **Featured:** St. Anton (village and Galzig), Galtür / Ischgl, Obergurgl,
  Obertauern, Saalbach, Nauders, Pitztal Glacier.
- **Climatology:** from each station's own record since 2000.
- **Hindcast gate:** 2026-02-01…14, models on the 8 featured stations,
  climatology and persistence on all 22.

### Germany: DWD precipitation stations (Bavarian Alps)

The German stations (`:DE:DWD`, `truth_source: dwd`) score against
`NSH_TAG`, the new snow an observer measures at the morning reading. It comes
from DWD's daily precipitation-station dataset (`more_precip`, `recent` and
`historical` zips; Deutscher Wetterdienst open data, CC BY 4.0). No key.
Automatic stations such as Garmisch-Partenkirchen record only snow depth and
are not used.

- **Candidates:** active stations at ≥ 600 m in the German Alps (47.2–47.95° N,
  9.8–13.2° E: Allgäu to Berchtesgaden). Kept when last winter (Nov–Mar) has
  ≥ 100 new-snow values and ≥ 10 days of 2 cm or more: 52 of 105.
- **Day attribution:** the value dated D is the morning-of-D measurement, so day
  D is the value dated D+1. Not fitted per station. All 85 German stations
  ≥ 700 m with new-snow data correlate best with the precipitation DWD files
  under the previous day (winter 2025/26), so the documented reading holds
  everywhere.
- **Featured:** 12 stations within 6 km of a ski area with 6+ lifts
  (Mittenwald, Oberammergau, Nesselwang, Pfronten, Reit im Winkl, Lenggries…).
- **Climatology:** from each station's own record since 2000.

### Italy: Meteotrentino snow fields (Trentino)

The Trentino stations (`:IT:METEOTN`, `truth_source: meteotn`) score against
HN, the 24-hour new snow an observer reads off a snow board at about 08:00 at
Meteotrentino's snow fields (AINEVA "Modello 1" observations). It comes from
Meteotrentino's open web service (`dati.meteotrentino.it`,
`rilieviNeveStagionaliUnaStazioneJson`, one request per field and season; also
listed on dati.trentino.it). Provincia Autonoma di Trento, CC BY 4.0. No key.

- **Codes:** HN is read from the raw AINEVA code (`000` and `0` = none, `015` =
  15 cm, `///` = missing). The service's numeric `HNnum` turns `0` into 1 cm on
  dry days, so it is not used. A reading after a day without an observer covers
  several days (`CheckCum` / `HnCum`) and is not scored.
- **Candidates:** all 55 fields in `listaCampiNeve`. Kept when last winter
  (Dec–Mar) has ≥ 80 single-day HN values and ≥ 10 days of 2 cm or more: 12
  of 55. Many fields are only staffed in some seasons.
- **Day attribution:** HN read on the morning of R matches the field's own
  depth rise into R (correlation 0.50–0.98 at Tonale, Buffaure, Pejo, Valles,
  2024–26) and not the rise after R (about 0), so day D is the value dated
  D+1, as for SLF and DWD.
- **Featured:** 7 fields within 4 km of a ski area with 10+ lifts (Passo
  Tonale, Folgarida, Canazei Belvedere, Buffaure, Passo Rolle, San Pellegrino,
  Passo Valles).
- **Climatology:** from each field's own record since 2005.

### Austria: Land Tirol snow-depth stations (Tyrol)

The Tyrolean stations (`:AT:LWDT`, `truth_source: lwdtirol`) are the automatic
snow-depth sensors of Land Tirol's avalanche warning service (LWD Tirol) and
hydrographic service (HD Tirol), many of them inside ski areas. The data is
"Wetterstationsdaten Tirol" on data.gv.at (Land Tirol, CC BY 4.0), read from
the SMET files behind lawinen.report (`wiski.tirol.gv.at/lawine/grafiken/smet`).
No key. Stations run by third parties (power companies, lift operators,
universities) appear on the same map but are not under that licence and are
not used.

- **Truth:** new snow is derived from depth, like SNOTEL: the depth at local
  midnight (median of the 10-minute readings 22:40–23:20 UTC) on D+1 minus the
  depth at midnight on D, counted as 0 below 1 cm (sensor jitter). Settling
  snow reads a little low on long storms, as for every depth-derived network.
  Midnight is taken at 23:00 UTC all season (CET); in late-season CEST weeks
  the day starts an hour after local midnight.
- **History:** the `winter` files hold the last 12 months of 10-minute data.
  Older days exist only as 24-hour means (`all` files, 1990s on): the
  climatology uses those, which spread a storm over two days but keep its
  total (82% of the 10-minute total at Rettenbachferner, same-day correlation
  0.74, winter 2025/26).
- **Candidates:** LWD/HD Tirol stations with a snow-depth sensor. Kept when
  last winter (Dec–Mar) has ≥ 100 days with both midnight depths and ≥ 10
  days of 2 cm or more: 71 (two co-located HD duplicates of LWD stations
  dropped).
- **Featured:** for each ski area with 15+ lifts (OpenSkiData), its nearest
  kept station within 4 km: 12 (Sölden, Stubai Glacier, Obergurgl, St. Anton,
  Hintertux, Mayrhofen, Serfaus-Fiss-Ladis, Axamer Lizum, Nordkette, Alpbach,
  Kals-Matrei, Stubai). One per area, so Sölden's four stations cost one
  Open-Meteo point.
- **Licence condition:** Land Tirol's open-data terms ask users to tell
  opendata@tirol.gv.at about applications built on the data.

### Italy: South Tyrol snow-depth stations (Alto Adige)

The South Tyrol stations (`:IT:PROVBZ`, `truth_source: provbz`) are the
automatic snow-depth (HS) sensors of the provincial weather service, most on
high pastures and ski slopes of the Dolomites and the Ötztal, Ortler and
Zillertal ranges. The data is "Misure meteo e idrografiche" on
data.civis.bz.it (Provincia autonoma di Bolzano – Alto Adige, CC0 1.0), read
from `geoservices.buergernetz.bz.it/services/meteo/v1/timeseries` (10-minute
readings, local time, one request per station per season). No key.

- **Truth:** computed exactly as for Land Tirol (same 22:40–23:20 UTC
  midnight window, 1 cm deadband and QC), so the two networks agree on what a
  day is. Checked against Meteotrentino's hand-measured new snow 9–12 km away
  (Karerpass and Obereggen vs Lusia, Piz La Ila vs Belvedere, Dec 2025–Mar
  2026): same-day correlation 0.63–0.73, lower at a one-day shift either way,
  so the days line up; totals are 60–75% of the hand-measured board, the
  settlement every depth-derived network shows.
- **History:** 10-minute data from the 2014/15 winter on, so the climatology
  uses the same 10-minute series as live truth.
- **Candidates:** all 26 stations with an HS sensor. Kept with the Tirol
  rule (≥ 100 valid days and ≥ 10 days of 2 cm or more last winter): 24
  (Ladurns had 98 valid days; Gantkofel reported nothing).
- **Featured:** for each ski area with 10+ lifts (OpenSkiData, the
  Trentino threshold), its nearest kept station within 4 km: 7 (Alta Badia,
  Sulden, Schöneben, 3 Zinnen Dolomites, Latemar/Obereggen, Carezza,
  Ratschings-Jaufen).

### Italy: ARPA Lombardia snow-depth sensors (Lombardy)

The Lombard stations (`:IT:ARPAL`, `truth_source: arpal`) are ARPA
Lombardia's automatic snow-depth sensors (nivometri), from Livigno and Bormio
to the Bergamo and Brescia Prealps. The data is CC0 1.0 on dati.lombardia.it
(Socrata, no key) in four datasets with one schema: up to 2010 (`5kig-ayub`),
2011–2020 (`id9e-7wf3`), 2021 on (`uqbu-tt6m`, validated, a few months
behind) and the recent months (`647i-nhxk`, live). Only the readings near
midnight are requested.

- **Truth:** depth at local midnight is the median of the valid (`stato` VA)
  readings from 23:00 to 00:50 local solar time (CET); day D is the rise to
  the next midnight, with the shared depth QC. The sensors report whole
  centimetres and jitter by 1–2 cm, so rises under 2 cm count as 0 (1 cm for
  Tirol and South Tyrol). Last season is re-read daily until a year old,
  because the validated archive fills in months late (March 2026 is not in
  either dataset yet).
- **Day check:** against Swiss manual new snow (SLF) last winter, Livigno La
  Vallaccia vs Berninapass correlates 0.59 same-day (0.40 and 0.25 shifted)
  and Alpe Motta vs Innerferrera 0.62 (0.04, 0.27): the pattern expected from
  a midnight day against an 08:00 board.
- **Candidates:** the 50 active snow-depth sensors in ARPA's register. Kept
  when either of the last two winters' Dec–Feb has ≥ 75 valid days and ≥ 8
  days of 2 cm or more: 32 (two winters, so the sensors installed in autumn
  2025 at Bormio 2000 and Livigno Mottolino qualify).
- **Featured:** for each ski area with 10+ lifts, its nearest kept station
  within 4 km: 8 (Livigno, Bormio, San Colombano, Aprica, Madesimo,
  Valmalenco, Foppolo-Carona, Piani di Bobbio). Bormio 2000 and Livigno
  Mottolino started in autumn 2025, after the climatology window (to June
  2025), so until their climatology can be built they count in MAE but drop
  out of the Powder Accuracy comparison for every team alike. No older
  sensor sits within 4 km of either ski area.

### Switzerland: MeteoSwiss manual stations (ski villages and valleys)

The MeteoSwiss stations (`:CH:MCH`, `truth_source: meteoswiss`) are manual
precipitation stations with a new-snow board in the ski villages and valleys
below the SLF study plots: Engelberg, Grindelwald, Kandersteg, Lenk,
Pontresina, Samedan, Sedrun, Crans-Montana, Leukerbad, Grächen, Saas Balen,
Säntis and more. The data is MeteoSwiss's open data (`ogd-nime`, CC BY 4.0,
"Source: MeteoSwiss"), one CSV per station on data.geo.admin.ch: the record
to last year (re-read once a year) and this year (re-read at most once per
UTC day, as MeteoSwiss asks). No key.

- **Truth:** `hns000d0`, the new snow (cm) read by hand at 06 UTC, the same
  board measurement as SLF's study plots. MeteoSwiss dates the 06–06 UTC
  reading by its first day, so day D takes the value dated D (offset 0; SLF
  dates it by the morning it is read, offset 1). Checked at every kept
  station last winter: the value dated D tracks the station's own depth rise
  from D to D+1 (correlation 0.90–1.00) and not the rise from D-1 to D
  (0.02–0.55).
- **Candidates:** the active manual stations with `hns000d0` at 800 m or
  higher and within 10 km of a ski area with 5+ lifts. 16 sit within 1 km of
  an SLF or IMIS station (Adelboden, Andermatt, Arosa, Davos, Gstaad,
  Weissfluhjoch…, often the same plot) and are dropped: one site, one truth.
  Kept when last winter (Nov–Mar) has ≥ 120 readings and ≥ 10 days of 2 cm or
  more: 58.
- **Tier:** all network. They score in the league's network board; none is
  featured, so they add no Open-Meteo points.

## Ground truth: JMA snow-depth sensors (japan league)

The japan league (`:JP:JMA`, `truth_source: jma`) scores against
**降雪の深さの合計**, JMA's daily snowfall total: the sum of each hour's rise
of the automatic snow-depth sensor over the JST calendar day. It comes from
JMA's past-weather archive (気象庁 過去の気象データ検索), one page per
station-month. Yesterday's preliminary value is there by the next JST morning,
and there's no key. Terms: the JMA website terms of use (compatible with
CC BY 4.0). Credit: 出典：気象庁ホームページ (Japan Meteorological Agency).

- **What it measures:** a depth sensor, not a snowboard. On long storms
  settling eats into the hourly rises, so totals read a little lower than an
  observer's board would. Every team is scored on the same number.
- **Value marks:** `)` (quasi-normal, a few hourly values missing) is kept.
  `]` (insufficient data), `#` (suspect), `×`, `///` and blanks are missing.
  `--` (no phenomenon) is 0.
- **Day attribution:** none to fit. The total is for the station's own local
  (JST) day, so day D is the value dated D.
- **Candidates:** every AMeDAS station that reports snow depth in JMA's live
  map, matched to its archive page by name (else the nearest page within
  3 km). A station is kept when last winter (Dec–Mar) has ≥ 100 days with a
  value, ≥ 10 snowfall days of 2 cm or more, and ≥ 80% of those snowfall days
  wet on the station's own rain gauge. That last test catches dead or stuck
  sensors with no external model call.
- **Featured:** stations within 8 km of a ski area with 5+ lifts, plus Sukayu (Hakkoda)
  (OpenSkiData, `data/skiareas/ski_areas_global.csv`).
- **Climatology:** from each station's own record since 2015-10. Jun–Sep are
  not fetched; they are snow-free at every kept station and outside the season.
- **Regional baseline:** `baseline-jma` (JMA MSM 5 km + GSM via Open-Meteo).

## Ground truth: morning snow depth, Sweden, Finland and Norway (nordic league)

The nordic league scores against new snow derived from the daily morning
snow-depth reading at Swedish, Finnish and Norwegian weather stations: day D is
max(depth at 06 UTC on D+1 − depth at 06 UTC on D, 0), the rise from one
morning (07:00 CET / 08:00 EET) to the next. Settling makes long storms read a
little low and melt days read 0, like SNOTEL depth truth. Every team is scored
on the same number. At 15 stations the value dated D is read at the end of
day D, so their day D is the rise D−1 → D (`day_offset` 0; see the NWP gate
below).

- **Sweden, SMHI** (`:SE:SMHI`, `truth_source: smhi`): parameter 8, snow depth
  once a day at 06 UTC, mostly read by observers. Corrected archive plus
  `latest-months` (yesterday's reading is in by the morning). Open data,
  CC BY 4.0, credit SMHI. 48 stations.
- **Finland, FMI** (`:FI:FMI`, `truth_source: fmi`): daily `snow` from FMI's
  WFS (`fmi::observations::weather::daily::simple`; −1 = no snow cover = 0).
  Open data, CC BY 4.0, credit Finnish Meteorological Institute. 45 stations.
  FMI's station list has no elevation, so elevations come from the Copernicus
  DEM via Open-Meteo's elevation API.
- **Norway, MET Norway** (`:NO:FROST`, `truth_source: frost`): element
  `surface_snow_thickness`, daily at 06 UTC (time offset PT6H), from the
  Frost API. Most stations are read by observers. Frost needs a free client
  id (`FROST_CLIENT_ID`, sent as the basic-auth user; an Actions secret for
  the resolve and baselines jobs). Licensed CC BY 4.0 / NLOD (each response
  cites CC BY 3.0 NO), credit MET Norway. 80 stations.
  - Readings with qualityCode 5 (very uncertain), 6 (model data) or 7
    (erroneous) are not used. Codes 0–4 are kept; the last few days are
    always 2 or 4 (not yet controlled).
  - **Drift check:** a rise of 10 cm or more is voided (`no_swe_support`)
    when the station's own gauge recorded under 0.5 mm over the same 24 h.
    The gauge value dated D covers the 24 h ending at 06 UTC D. Over winter
    2025-26 it correlates 0.39 with the depth rise D−1 → D and 0.03 with
    D → D+1, across 208 stations. A missing gauge reading voids nothing.
  - **Candidates:** Norwegian stations (not Svalbard) within 25 km of a ski
    area or at ≥ 400 m. Kept on the shared gate below. The series must
    also have started by October 2022 (≥ 3 winters of climatology), and the
    station must have reported on ≥ 70% of the last 14 days. That keeps 136
    of 282. The rest: 75 had too few snow days (mostly summits and coast),
    31 too few valid days, 29 are not reporting this November, and 11
    series are too new. The NWP gate then keeps 80 of the 136.
  - **Gate:** Norway is noisier than Sweden and Finland: median r 0.39
    against 0.55–0.63.
    - 54 stations fail, mostly in the western fjords and mountains. There
      ECMWF has about twice the snow the depth record shows: wet snow
      settles fast and rain falls on snow. Tryvannshøgda (Oslo) is among
      them.
    - The dating differs by observer. 85 stations line up on the standard
      rise D → D+1, and 45 a day earlier. The 21 kept stations where the
      earlier day wins by 0.1 take `day_offset` 0. Hemsedal Hølto is one:
      r 0.52 against 0.39.
    - Meråker and Tågdalen line up a day *later*. A morning reading cannot
      do that, so they are treated as misdated and dropped.
- **Candidates:** active stations within 25 km of a ski area, at ≥ 400 m
  (Sweden) or north of 66° N (Finland). Kept when last winter (Nov–Mar) has
  ≥ 120 valid days and ≥ 10 days of 2 cm or more.
- **QC:** missing either reading, negative or > 300 in depth, or a rise of
  more than 48 in voids the day. Big jumps have no SWE or precipitation
  cross-check: many SMHI stations have no co-located gauge (Storlien's ends in
  2023), and the readings are manual.
- **NWP gate (2026-11-20):** each station's daily truth over winter 2025-26
  (2025-12-01..2026-03-15) is correlated with the direct ECMWF IFS daily
  snowfall for the same local day, and with the truth shifted a day either way
  (`scripts/nwp_gate.py`). The decisions are stored in `data/gates/nordic.csv`
  and applied by the three generators (`data/gates/README.md`), so a
  regenerated list keeps them.
  - **Re-dated:** 15 stations (11 FMI, mostly synoptic and airport sensors,
    and 4 SMHI: Kindsjön D, Mörsil, Orsa D, Särna D) line up a day earlier by
    a wide margin. For example, Kindsjön D gives r 0.77 against 0.26 and
    Rovaniemi airport 0.55 against 0.33. They now take day D as the rise
    D−1 → D (`day_offset` 0) and their climatology was rebuilt.
  - **Dropped:** 8 stations whose best shift stays below r 0.4. Four are FMI:
    Kilpisjärvi, Angeli, Nellim and Tulppio. Four are SMHI: Abisko Aut,
    Kittelfjäll D, Letafors D and Ljusnedal.
  - The other 78 pass (median r 0.56).
  - The 2026-02-01..14 featured hindcast before these changes: AIFS 10.5,
    consensus 9.9, ECMWF 6.3, GFS 3.6, climatology 0.
- **Featured:** 14 Swedish and Finnish stations within 12 km of a ski area
  with 3+ lifts (Sälen, Höglekardalen, Saariselkä, Paljakka, Salla…), plus 3
  Norwegian ones, which replace Abisko, Kittelfjäll and Ljusnedal
  (Funäsdalen) after the gate. The Norwegian three are the nearest kept
  station that passes the gate for each of the three biggest areas: Hemsedal
  (Hølto, 2 km), Oppdal (Maurhaugen, 9 km) and Skimore Oslo (Brunkollen,
  7 km). The featured count stays at 17, so the Open-Meteo budget does not
  rise.
- **Climatology:** from each station's own record since 2010 (Norway's
  80: from Frost history, each at its gated `day_offset`).

## Ground truth: weather-station snow depth and Czech observers (world league)

The world league is for snowy mountains that the national networks above do
not reach. It starts small, with 11 stations whose truth is online within
days: the Tatras, the Czech mountains, Sljeme above Zagreb and Palandöken
(Erzurum). Every source here is free, needs no key and allows reuse. It grows
as national feeds are added: Norway needs MET Norway's Frost (a free client
id), Spain AEMET (a free key), and Poland and Slovenia need their own archives.
Slovakia's SHMÚ open-data server (opendata.shmu.sk, CC BY 4.0, no
registration) is the next candidate. It publishes a 1-minute snow-depth
average for 95 automatic stations every 5 minutes, with about 30 days kept
online. Its daily climate files carry measured new snow (`sneh_novy`, 07–07
local time), but they arrive months late and go back only to January 2025,
too short for a climatology so far.

- **WMO weather stations, via NOAA GHCNh** (`:GHCNH`, `truth_source: ghcnh`):
  the morning snow-depth group of the stations' SYNOP reports (06 UTC in
  Europe), decoded by NOAA NCEI into GHCNh, the successor of ISD, on AWS Open
  Data (`noaa-ghcnh-pds`, one Parquet per station per year, no key). NODD
  data is CC0 1.0, and WMO's Unified Data Policy (Resolution 1, 2021) makes
  these reports core data, free to reuse. Day D is max(morning depth on D+1 −
  morning depth on D, 0), counted from 1 cm, with the shared depth QC: the
  nordic league's rule.
  - **Morning hour:** fitted per station, the earliest synoptic hour 05–11
    local solar time with at least 80% of the reports of the best one (06
    UTC everywhere kept).
  - **Junk:** the live feed carries off-hour spikes (Lomnický štít read
    2,340 mm at 07 UTC between 220 and 210 mm) and morning ones (10 mm on a
    1,000 mm pack). A reading counts only when a neighbouring synoptic
    reading agrees within max(25 cm, 25%).
  - **Bare ground:** many countries leave the depth group out when there is
    no snow. A morning report without it reads 0 cm only if one of these
    holds: the last confirmed morning depth, within 20 days, was ≤ 5 cm; or
    the station sent no depth for 20 days but did last winter (within 300
    days) or does within 45 days. It never reads 0 right before a morning
    above 60 cm. Under a pack it stays missing, and so does a stretch of
    years with no depth group at all (several Norwegian stations before
    2014–2017).
  - **Dropouts:** some stations file 0 when the depth cannot be read
    (Kasprowy Wierch filed 0 on 32 mornings of Dec 2025–Feb 2026 under 70+
    cm; Kredarica 14). A 0 between mornings of ≥ 20 cm on both sides within
    5 days is dropped, and a station with more than 3 last winter is left
    out: its zeros cannot be told from bare ground. That leaves out most
    Romanian, Serbian, Slovenian and Polish mountain stations.
  - **Units by source:** GHCNh merges feeds.
    - The WMO SYNOP feed (source 220) is in mm. Its special values are
      scaled too: 9970 (< 0.5 cm) and 9980 (patchy cover) read 0, and 9990
      is missing.
    - MET Norway's own hourly feed (398) is in cm, with −1 for no snow.
      Before about 2023 it is the only depth source for most Norwegian
      stations; read as mm, their history came out ten times too dry.
    - Poland's IMGW feed (395) files 0 at every hour it did not observe, so
      it is not used.
  - **Latency:** the year files run two days behind, so the league resolves
    at D+6. They are not complete in real time, though: NOAA backfills some
    feeds months later. On 2026-11-19 the Norwegian and Spanish stations had
    a morning report on only 3–8 of the past 13 days, and every station was
    missing 10–12 November.
  - **Candidates:** GHCNh stations outside the countries with a national
    network here, at ≥ 400 m (or north of 60° N) and within 12 km of a ski
    area with 3+ lifts. The OpenSkiData global extract now covers 41 more
    countries. A candidate is kept when it passes all of these:
    - last winter (Nov–Mar) has ≥ 100 valid days and ≥ 8 days of 2 cm or
      more;
    - at ≥ 1,200 m, snow lies on ≥ 50% of Dec–Feb mornings;
    - ≤ 3 dropouts;
    - a morning report on ≥ 70% of the last 14 days, as read in real time;
    - ≥ 3 such winters inside the climatology window;
    - the per-station NWP gate (below).

    That keeps 3: Telgárt (Slovakia), and Erzurum and Erzurum Bölge (Turkey).
    The 12 Norwegian
    stations and Navacerrada pass everything but the real-time test. Central
    Asia and Korea report too sparsely (Shymbulak had 2 snow days on record
    last winter).
- **Czech observers, ČHMÚ** (`:CZ:CHMI`, `truth_source: chmi`): element
  `SNO`, the new snow an observer measures at 07:00 CET, from ČHMÚ's open
  climate data (opendata.chmi.cz, CC BY 4.0, credit ČHMÚ, no key): one
  historical CSV per station and element, then one JSON per month. Readings
  flagged `A` (affected by snowmaking) or with quality ≥ 3 are not used.
  - **Dating:** the reading is filed under the day the snow fell. The offset
    is fitted per station against its own morning depth (`SCE`): 0 where the
    value dated D tracks the depth rise D → D+1, 1 where it tracks D−1 → D,
    and the station is left out when neither wins by 0.1. Every kept station
    fits 0: the rise D → D+1 correlates 0.94–0.98 with the reading, D−1 → D
    only 0.14–0.78.
  - **Latency:** the professional stations' readings are online the next
    morning. The volunteer climate observers' readings arrive with the
    monthly report, six to eight weeks late (on 2026-11-19 October was still
    blank), so a kept station's latest reading must be at most 7 days old.
    Before a month closes, some stations file SNO only on mornings with new
    snow. A blank SNO reads 0 when the next morning's depth is 0 cm; under a
    pack it stays missing.
  - **Kept** when last winter has ≥ 120 readings and ≥ 10 days of 2 cm or
    more, the readings are current, and the station has ≥ 3 winters of
    record in the climatology window. That gives 5: Pec pod Sněžkou,
    Churáňov, Klínovec, Hojsova Stráž and Desná–Souš.
    - 17 more volunteer stations would qualify except for the reporting
      delay, among them Benecko, Kvilda and Špičák.
    - Stations that now report only automatic depth, with no observer new
      snow, are left out: Luční bouda and Labská bouda (since November
      2023), Harrachov (2021), Šerák (2025), Jizerka (2000) and eight more.
    - Praděd's observer record stops in 1998 and resumes in 2025.
- **NWP gate (2026-11-20):** each station's daily truth over winter 2025-26
  (2025-12-01..2026-03-15, ~100 days) is correlated with the direct ECMWF IFS
  daily snowfall for the same local day, and with the truth shifted a day
  either way (`scripts/nwp_gate.py`). A station needs r ≥ 0.4 at its best
  shift.
  - **Kept:** the ČHMÚ observers at r 0.65–0.76 (all best at shift 0),
    Erzurum Bölge 0.73, Telgárt 0.59, Erzurum 0.56.
  - **Dropped:** the summit stations Chopok (r 0.27) and Lomnický štít
    (0.30): wind, not snowfall, moves their depth. Their "new snow" ran 1.3×
    and 2.4× the model's. Also Puntijarka (0.01 same day, 0.37 a day later;
    4× the model's total).
  - The 2026-02-01..14 hindcast gate before the drop: ECMWF 1.2 against
    climatology 0, consensus −17. The summit truth was the reason.
- **Featured:** for each ski area with 10+ lifts, its nearest kept station
  within 6 km (GHCNh, also no more than 150 m below the area's lowest lift)
  or 5 km (ČHMÚ). That gives 3: Pec pod Sněžkou, Skiarena Jizerky (Desná) and
  Skiareál Klínovec. The Tatras' featured stations were the two summits.
- **Climatology:** from each station's own record since 2000.

## Ground truth: ERA5 analysis (era5 league)

There is no southern-hemisphere SNOTEL equivalent with a stable public API
(we probed: NIWA is key-gated with negotiated access; Chile's DGA is
session-based JSP portals; the CL/AR Observatorio Andino is an R Shiny
websocket app). The era5 league therefore scores against **ERA5 reanalysis
daily snowfall** at each resort's coordinates, fetched from Open-Meteo's
keyless archive API (`archive-api.open-meteo.com`, CC BY 4.0).

Honest caveats, disclosed everywhere the league appears:

- ERA5 is a **model analysis**, not a snow stake. At ~25 km grid scale it
  mutes maritime peaks (NZ/AU totals read low) and misses microclimates.
  High-resolution models over-forecast relative to ERA5's smoothed totals —
  calibrating your forecasts to the truth's scale is part of the game (July
  2025 hindcast: GFS +49 Powder Accuracy, the high-res best_match blend −34).
- Truth is pinned to pure reanalysis (`models=era5_seamless`). The archive's
  default "best_match" blends in the same operational data the forecast
  archive serves, which would make the NWP baseline literally identical to
  truth (we hit this: MAE 0.000 before pinning).
- ERA5 assimilates observations that also feed the live forecast models, so
  the NWP baselines are structurally correlated with the truth — treat their
  era5-league Powder Accuracy scores as a hard, slightly flattered target.
- The archive lags real time by ~5 days, so rounds resolve from D+8.
- QC is missing-data-only (no sensor glitches to void).

## Ground truth: resort snow reports (resorts league)

The resorts league scores against **what the mountain itself reports**: each
resort's published snow report, scraped from the resort's own site by
`src/powderbench/resortfeeds/` and archived in this repo.

Why this is its own league and never truth elsewhere: resort numbers are
marketing-adjacent — measured at generous stakes, rounded up, occasionally
corrected. That makes them unusable as truth for the stations/era5 leagues.
Inside a dedicated league the bias is harmless: every competitor is scored
against the same published number, and forecasting *what the resort will
report* is a legitimate, well-defined game. The `resorts` obs feed keeps the
receipts by publishing resort-claimed totals next to ERA5 reanalysis.

**The ephemerality problem.** A resort's "24h snowfall" disappears when
tomorrow's report replaces it, so truth can never be re-fetched. A scrape cron
(`.github/workflows/scrape-resorts.yml`, 20:30 UTC for Oceania mornings and
13:00 UTC for South America mornings) archives every report as an immutable
snapshot CSV under `data/resortreports/raw/`, failures included — the archive
is both the truth source and the public audit trail. Resolution reads only
the committed archive.

**Date attribution.** A report scraped between one morning refresh and the
next describes snow that fell on the *previous* local calendar day (the same
end-of-day convention as SNOTEL's `periodRef=END`):

```
attributed = (local_scrape_time − report_hour_local).date() − 1 day
```

Two scrapes landing on the same attributed day are deduped read-side keeping
the earliest (closest after the morning refresh). Resorts that publish only a
cumulative season total (e.g. NZSki) yield daily snowfall as day-over-day
deltas, clamped at zero — a day after an archive gap can't be attributed and
is voided.

**QC (resorts league):**

| Rule | Condition |
|---|---|
| `missing` | no archived report covers that station-day (scrape failed, site down, robots disallow) |
| `implausible_jump` | reported 24h total > 48" |

**Politeness and consent.** Aggregators (OnTheSnow, Snow-Forecast, …) are
never scraped — their ToS prohibit it. Only individual resort sites are used,
each onboarded with a robots.txt + terms review recorded in the registry's
`verified` stamp (`src/powderbench/resortfeeds/registry.py`); the scraper
re-checks robots.txt on every run, identifies itself with a descriptive
User-Agent (`PowderBench/0.1 (+https://powderbench.com; …)`), spaces requests
per host, and touches each site at most twice a day. If a resort objects, its
spec is disabled and its station voids from that day forward. Per-resort

## Climatology

`data/climatology/<league>.csv` — stations from water years 2016–2025 of
SNOTEL history; era5 from ERA5 1991–2025; resorts from ERA5 1991–2025 at the
resort coordinates, pending real report history (`powderbench
build-climatology --league <name>`). For each station × day-of-year (±7-day circular window,
pooled across years): mean, quantiles (p10–p90) of the 24h / 48h / 72h
snowfall distributions, and the empirical frequency of ≥6" days. The
**median** is climatology's point forecast (MAE-optimal no-skill reference).

## Official NWS baseline: WPC snowfall percentiles

`baseline-wpc` is NOAA WPC's Probabilistic Winter Precipitation Forecast — the
official NWS snowfall percentiles on the 2.5 km CONUS grid (public domain,
`ftp.wpc.ncep.noaa.gov/pwpf/conus_2.5km/`). From the 00Z cycle of round day D
(posted ~18 UTC the day before): h24 = the 24h window ending 06Z D+1, h48 = the
48h window ending 06Z D+2 (both within ~2h of mountain-local midnight), h72 =
the 72h window ending 00Z D+3 (WPC's longest; ~6h early). Point = median; the
quantile columns are WPC's 10/25/50/75/90th percentiles (negative tails clipped
to 0); `prob_6in` = WPC P(≥6") on h24. Nearest grid point within 5 km
(Alaska is off-grid). WPC keeps only ~1.5 days online, so the committed
baseline submissions double as the archive. Needs `pip install -e .[grib]`.

## Official NWS baseline: NDFD snow amount (the human forecasters)

`baseline-ndfd` is the snow amount NWS forecast offices publish in the National
Digital Forecast Database. It comes from the CONUS 2.5 km grid, in the public
domain, at `noaa-ndfd-pds` `wmo/snow/…/YSUZ98_KWBN_*`. The bucket refreshes it
about every 30 minutes and keeps the archive.

- **File used:** the newest file posted before the run, and never later than 3 h
  before the cutoff.
- **Windows:** the 6 h periods are summed over the WPC windows. h24 is 06Z D to
  06Z D+1, and h48 is 06Z D to 06Z D+2.
- **No h72:** the evening issuance doesn't reach 06Z D+3, so NDFD has no h72 forecast
  (a 24/48 h baseline, like HRRR).
- **Stations:** nearest grid point within 5 km. Alaska and Canada are off-grid.

Alignment check on the resolved stations rounds: 24 h windows starting 06Z and
12Z fit the truth best (MAE 1.39″ and 1.35″), against 1.55″ for 00Z and 1.62″
for 24Z.

## NWP baselines: Open-Meteo

[Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/) (CC BY 4.0, no key):

- **Timing**: baselines run 3 hours before each league's cutoff — GitHub's
  scheduler routinely starts jobs 30 min–3 h late, and a baseline must never
  see forecasts issued after the cutoff its competitors faced.
- **Live rounds**: `/v1/forecast` at each station's coordinates and elevation,
  daily `snowfall_sum`, models `best_match` (every station) and `gfs_seamless`
  (featured stations only — Open-Meteo bills per location, and the free daily
  budget can't cover several models × thousands of stations), submitted 45
  minutes before the cutoff.
- **Model roster** (featured stations, `baselines.MODEL_BASELINES`):
  `baseline-ecmwf` (ECMWF IFS 0.25°), `baseline-aifs` (ECMWF's AI model),
  `baseline-icon` (DWD), `baseline-gem` (Environment Canada), `baseline-ukmo`
  (Met Office); US leagues add `baseline-nbm` (NOAA National Blend) and
  `baseline-hrrr` (3 km, 48h range — scored on h24/h48 only); Alps leagues add
  `baseline-arome` (Météo-France); Japan adds `baseline-jma` (JMA MSM 5 km +
  GSM; snowfall confirmed 2026-11-19). Models that don't run in a region are
  skipped. KMA via Open-Meteo returned no usable snowfall (2026-10).
- **`baseline-consensus`**: the median of all NWP baselines per
  station-horizon (≥3 models) — the "beat the crowd" target.
- **Hindcast**: `historical-forecast-api.open-meteo.com` — the archive of what
  models actually predicted at the time (available from ~2021). Lead times in
  the archive are short, so hindcast NWP skill at 48h/72h is optimistic;
  live-round NWP baselines are the true benchmark.

## Layout of generated data

```
data/leagues.yaml                        league config
data/rounds/<league>/<D>/round.json      manifest: cutoff, stations, target days
data/rounds/<league>/<D>/truth.csv       QC'd truth written at resolution
data/submissions/<league>/<D>/<team>.csv all submissions for round D
data/results/<league>/rounds/<D>.json    per-team metrics + QC counts
data/results/<league>/leaderboard.json   season + last-30 aggregates
data/obs/<league>/                       observation-feed reference data
data/resortreports/raw/                  archived resort snow-report snapshots
data/cache/                              API response cache (gitignored)
```
