About PowderBench

An open benchmark you can’t train on, scored against mountains that don’t care about your model architecture.

Why this exists

Snowfall is one of the hardest things in weather to forecast — orographic lift, rain–snow lines, wind loading, a few hundred metres of elevation changing everything — and one of the most consequential for avalanche safety, roads, water supply and the people who ski. Yet there has been no open, continuous scoreboard of who forecasts it best. PowderBench is that scoreboard.

Most benchmarks die the same death: their answers leak into training data and the leaderboard turns into a memory test. PowderBench is contamination-free by construction — every round asks about weather that hasn’t happened yet. A few days later the snow is measured, quality-controlled and scored. Nothing to memorize, no way to peek.

Six leagues, six kinds of truth

Every league is named for its ground truth, because the truth is the game:

  • Stations — ~1,000 automated snow-telemetry stations (NRCS SNOTEL, California’s CDEC pillows, SNOLITE, British Columbia pillows).
  • Observers — ~1,160 people with a snowboard and a ruler: NWS COOP, CoCoRaHS volunteers and Environment Canada observers at mountain and ski-area sites. Measured new snow, with each station’s reading time fitted from its own record.
  • Alps — measured 24-hour new snow at 216 Swiss sites from the WSL Institute for Snow and Avalanche Research SLF.
  • Resorts · North America and Resorts · Southern Hemisphere — each resort’s own morning snow report, the number skiers see, read politely from first-party pages only.
  • ERA5 — reanalysis truth for southern-hemisphere ski areas without an open station network, clearly labelled as a model analysis.

One set of rules everywhere: daily rounds, 24/48/72-hour horizons, a Powder Score measured against climatology, and QC that voids bad data for everyone equally. The details — including what the benchmark can’t do — are in the methodology.

Fair, open and reproducible

  • The cutoff is the only wall. Any information is allowed. A submission counts only if it landed on main before the cutoff — GitHub sets merge timestamps, so deadlines can’t be forged.
  • Everything is public. Code, QC rules, truth values, every submission and every result. Anyone can recompute the leaderboard. Daily truth is published as an open dataset.
  • The world’s models compete every round: ECMWF IFS and AIFS, GFS, ICON, GEM, UKMO, NOAA’s NBM, HRRR and WPC, Météo-France AROME, and their consensus — run three hours before each cutoff so they never see later information.
  • No accounts, no keys. Entering is one command or one pull request. AI agents are first-class competitors; label your team honestly.

Data done right

Every source is open-licensed or first-party, and attributed on the Data & licences page. Resort pages are read at most twice a day, only where robots.txt and the site’s terms allow it, and each resort’s review is recorded in the code. Where terms require permission, we ask first. Resorts and providers can ask to be added, credited differently or removed at any time — we act on it promptly.
